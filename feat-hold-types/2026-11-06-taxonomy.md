# Hold types: a taxonomy proposal (#63)

*2026-11-06, first version. For the maintainer's decision before any labelling at scale.*

[#63](https://github.com/CruxCoach/CruxCoach/issues/63) gives every hold on every supported board a type, a size,
the direction it is pulled, and for each label a confidence and its source. Statistics come later, on top of that
dataset. This proposal settles what one label contains: three options, a recommendation, and the questions that
need an answer before the first board is labelled.

## What a label can rest on

- **Board images.** Every supported board except Shiny Wall has a front view of each hold set in the app, as a
  render or a photo. The Kilter Original images have about 7.5 pixels per inch, so a 2 cm edge is about 6 pixels.
  - Visible: the outline and footprint of a hold, the side where it is thick or has a lip, texture, holes.
  - Not visible: how deep an edge is in millimetres, the fine detail of an incut, anything behind a lip.
- **Hold sets.** Some sets are for feet by design (kickboards, foot sets). Several boards are mirror-symmetric, so
  most hold models sit there twice, mirrored.
- **Positions.** Every placement has x/y. The Aurora-family boards store no rotation, so the orientation of each
  placement is read from the image.
- **Hold models.** Where the same model sits on many placements, it is labelled once and only its orientation is
  read per placement.
- **Shiny Wall** has no board image in the app. It comes last in the order and will need another source, to be
  settled then.

So: type and orientation can be read for most holds; size only as a coarse visual class. Edge depth in millimetres
cannot be measured from these images, and any number for it would be false precision.

## Three options

### A: small, the words of the manual tags

- **Type:** jug, crimp, sloper, pinch, pocket, volume (the manual log tags without "undercling").
- **Direction:** down-pull, sidepull left, sidepull right, undercling, or all round.
- **Size:** none.
- **Two uses:** one type per hold.
- **Foot holds:** not marked.

Fast and consistent, and it maps one to one onto the manual tags. But it cannot answer the open questions in #63:
no crimp sizes, two-use holds lose their second use, and tiny foot chips are forced into a hand type.

### B: medium, with second use, size classes and foot holds (recommended)

Per hold model:

- **Type**, one primary: jug, edge (crimp), sloper, pinch, pocket, volume, and **foot chip** for holds too small
  to use with a hand.
- **Second use**, optional: one more type when a hold is clearly used two ways, e.g. a pinch with a good edge or a
  jug with a pocket.
- **Size**, three classes whose meaning depends on the type:

  | Type | small | medium | large |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | edge | less than one finger pad | about one pad | more than one pad |
  | pinch | narrow | medium | wide |
  | pocket | one or two fingers | three fingers | four fingers or more |
  | jug, sloper, volume | small footprint | medium | large |

  In addition, every hold gets its measured footprint (width × height in cm) from the image.
- **Use:** hand and foot (the default) or foot only (kickboards, foot sets, foot chips).

Per placement:

- **Direction:** the side the usable part of the hold faces, in eight steps (up, up-right, right, … ), or "all
  round" for holds that work from every side. The stats group it into down-pull (facing up), sidepull (facing
  sideways) and undercling (facing down). A gaston is not a property of the hold: it is a sidepull pushed away
  from the body, which depends on the hand and the move, so it is left to a later per-move analysis.

Per label (type, second use, size and direction each):

- **Confidence** from 0 to 1, shown as high, medium or low. Everything below high goes on the review list.
- **Source:** set rule, visual label, copied from the same hold model, measured from the image, confirmed in
  review, or part of the reference set.

It covers the open questions in #63 (crimp size, foot-only holds, volumes) at the precision the images support.
The cost is more labelling work than A, and edge size is an estimate. Its accuracy is measured against the
reference set like every other field.

### C: fine, with subtypes and millimetres

- **Type:** about fifteen subtypes, e.g. incut, flat and slopey edge, rail, micro edge, foot chip, narrow and wide
  pinch, mono, two- and three-finger pocket, sloper, jug, horn, volume.
- **Size:** in millimetres (edge depth, pinch width).
- **Direction:** a free angle in degrees.
- **Two uses:** a weighted list of types per hold.

The richest base for the later send probability, but the images cannot show edge depth or fine incut. Many
subtypes would be guesses with low confidence, and people reviewing them would often disagree. Single fields of C
can be added on top of B later, for a board where measured data exists.

### Side by side

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Types | 6 | 7 + second use | ~15, weighted |
| Size | none | 3 classes + measured footprint | millimetres |
| Direction | 4 + all round | 8 + all round | degrees |
| Foot-only holds | no | yes | yes |
| Fits what the images show | yes | yes | partly |
| Labelling and review work | low | medium | high |
| Matches the manual tags | 1 : 1 | by a fixed mapping | by a fixed mapping |

## Holds with two uses

1. **One type only.** Simple, but a pinch that is also a good edge counts only as one of them.
2. **Primary and optional second use (recommended).** The stats count the primary type; the second use shows up in
   the hold-type filter and in the details of a problem.
3. **A weighted list.** The most precise, but weights are hard to label consistently, and the stats become harder
   to explain.

## Computed labels and manual tags

- **Kept apart.** Computed labels belong to the hold dataset: read-only, versioned, shipped with the app. Manual
  tags belong to the user's log. The app never writes one into the other, and the manual tags stay as they are.
- **Same words.** Each manual tag maps onto the taxonomy: crimp → edge, sloper → sloper, pinch → pinch, jug → jug,
  pocket → pocket, volume → volume, undercling → direction "facing down".
- **In the stats.** Board problems are counted by their computed labels. Logs without a board problem are counted
  by their manual tags. Where a board log also has manual tags, they are shown next to the computed ones ("you
  tagged: crimp") and do not replace them, so stats stay comparable across boards.
- **As a check.** A tag on a board log says "this problem has at least one hold of this type". On the phone that is
  an agreement check. It reaches the dataset only when someone reports it in the review screen; nothing is
  uploaded.

How the stats would use the labels, in short: the hand holds of a problem are its start, middle and finish holds;
foot holds and foot-only holds count separately. A problem's dominant type is the primary type of most of its hand
holds. The stats themselves are a separate proposal, after the Kilter dataset.

## Recommendation

**Option B**, with primary type and optional second use, three size classes plus the measured footprint, foot-only
holds marked, direction in eight steps, and confidence and source for every field.

## Next, once the taxonomy is settled

1. A first pass on part of the Kilter board, then a reference set of about 150 holds across types and boards for
   the maintainer to check. Accuracy is measured against it, per field.
2. All Kilter layouts and sets, with a list of uncertain holds for review.
3. A proposal for how the dataset reaches the app, and a review screen in a test build where anyone can see a hold
   highlighted on the board and confirm or correct its label.
4. MoonBoard, Tension, Grasshopper, Decoy, So iLL, Touchstone and Shiny Wall, in that order.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. Option A, B or C? Recommended: B.
2. Edge size: three visual classes by finger pad, or no edge size for now? Recommended: three classes, with their
   accuracy measured.
3. Foot-only holds (kickboards, foot sets, foot chips): mark them and leave them out of the hand profile?
   Recommended: yes.
4. Volumes: a type of their own, labelled where a board has them? Recommended: yes; few boards have any.
5. Two uses: primary type and an optional second use? Recommended: yes.
6. A board log with manual tags: show the tags next to the computed labels, or let them replace the computed labels
   for that log? Recommended: show next to them.
7. May the labelling also use how often a hold appears as a start, hand, finish or foot hold in the catalogue's
   problems, as an extra signal for foot-only holds and for the confidence? Only counts, used while labelling;
   nothing about problems goes into the dataset. Recommended: yes, as a tie-breaker only.
