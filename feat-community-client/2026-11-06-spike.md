# Community in the app: spike report (#61)

2026-11-07. Branch `cruxcoach-agent:feat/community-client`. Protocol map:
[`docs/community/PROTOCOL.md`](https://github.com/cruxcoach-agent/CruxCoach/blob/feat/community-client/docs/community/PROTOCOL.md).

## Summary

The spike works: with the key the app already has, a phone joins the community with an invite link,
accepts the rules, lists every room and forum, and reads and writes messages, thread replies, forum
topics and comments live against the real server. Once the phone has looked up the server's address,
the first room list is there in 0.9 s. The protocol core is plain Kotlin in `shared` with 29 unit
tests, and an integration test runs every pull request against the pinned server version.

The work showed where the cost is: not in the protocol, which is small and well documented, but in
keeping up with upstream (about 29 commits a day) and in the parts a chat app needs around it
(media, notifications without Google, an offline cache). Recommendation: go on one phase at a time,
starting with phase 1 behind the same preview switch.

## What works

The Community preview is a separate part of the app, in feature and development builds only, and off until
the user turns it on.

- **Sign in** with the app's own key or Amber. The client answers the relay's NIP-42 challenge before any
  read; it checks every event's signature, and accepts relay-only events (room lists, member lists,
  thread summaries) only from the relay's own key.
- **Join** with an invite link, pasted or opened from `community.cruxcoach.org/invite/…`: the app shows the
  community rules and the privacy notice, asks for the age confirmation when the community requires it,
  then claims the invite. It does not join any room on its own.
- **Room list** with rooms and forums, the rooms you are in first, open rooms you can join below them;
  announce rooms (read-only for new members) are marked.
- **Room timeline** with live updates and sending. History comes from the relay's top-level window (only
  thread heads, with reply counts), so busy threads do not push the room's own messages out of view.
- **Threads** with replies, live. **Forums**: topics, comment counts, new topics, comments.
- Names from profiles; people without a profile show as a short npub, your own messages as "You".
  Edits, deletions and reactions from other apps are shown, in the room and inside threads; room
  notices (someone joined, the topic changed) are one quiet line.
- The connection closes a minute after the app leaves the screen and comes back when you return.
  Nothing runs in the background.

Tests: 29 unit tests in `shared` (parsing with event shapes from Buzz's documentation, templates, invite
links, rules, claim results, the session's sign-in, reconnect, publish and query logic, the relay-key
check, timeline merging), and an integration test that runs in CI against a real relay at the pinned
version.

**CI.** The integration test starts Buzz's own `deploy/compose` bundle (relay, Postgres, Redis, MinIO) at
the pinned version, with the image pinned by digest, and never talks to community.cruxcoach.org. An
owner key creates a room and a forum and mints an invite; a new key reads and accepts the community
rules, claims the invite, lists the rooms including the forum, joins both, receives the owner's message
live, posts, replies in the owner's thread (the reply stays out of the room timeline), sees the owner's
reaction on that reply in the open thread and in one opened later, and opens a forum topic that the
owner comments on. It passes on [#66](https://github.com/CruxCoach/CruxCoach/pull/66) in about 8 minutes,
most of it pulling images; the first room list came 0.12 s after connecting on the runner.

**Device check, build 2** (commit c6b9691), on a test phone against the real server, with a key the app
created for the test:

- Opening the invite link starts the join in the app. The community rules and the privacy notice show
  in full; accepting them claims the invite and opens the room list.
- The room list shows the rooms the key is in first, the open rooms with a Join button, and forums in
  their own section. The app joins no room on its own.
- Sending a message works and shows up live; a forum topic too.
- Three bugs, fixed in build 3:
  1. Room notices (room created, member joined) showed as raw JSON. They are now one quiet line
     ("Alice joined"), unknown notice types are hidden, and a notice counts only when the relay's own
     key signed it.
  2. A thread or forum topic opened without its first message, after a spinner of about 20 s.
  3. A thread reply went out as a normal message and showed up in the room timeline.

  Bugs 2 and 3 had one cause: the room screen handed the thread screen its arguments in a different
  order than the navigation route expected, so the thread screen got "stream" as the message id. The
  reply-building code now refuses anything that is not an event id, so this kind of mistake fails
  loudly instead of sending a malformed reply.

**Review.** Before the next device check, a fresh review of the whole branch, checked against the
relay's source, found nine more problems. All are fixed in build 5 (commit 0a7001f):

1. A thread never showed edits, deletions or reactions on its replies: those events name only the
   reply, and the thread view asked only for events that name the thread's first message.
2. Tapping a reply that had also been sent to the room opened a sub-thread of that reply, and the
   relay refused replies written there.
3. If the relay's key could not be fetched at the start, room lists and room notices were accepted
   from any signer for the rest of the session. They are now dropped until the key is known, and the
   key is fetched again until it arrives.
4. The connection stayed open in the background for as long as the app ran, and with Amber it kept
   trying to sign in without a screen to ask on.
5. Smaller ones: a refused sign-in left its socket open and counted twice for the retry delay; the
   room list could keep spinning after a cancelled refresh; a message that could not be sent was gone
   from the text field; following a read-only room failed silently; and server errors while joining
   were shown as "signing refused".

The new integration step (a reaction on a thread reply) then caught one more: a thread showed its
replies first and its first message a moment later. Build 6 (commit 10bf02c) waits for the first
message; its CI run passed, integration test included.

Build 4 (commit b801c9e) made names and links readable and keeps the accept button off until
the rules text is on screen (it rendered a moment after its card, so the button was there first).

**Device check, build 6** (commit 7b4016a; the same code as 10bf02c, pushed again because the build

## What was hard

- **Two transports.** Room history that hides thread replies (NIP-CW windows) exists only on the HTTP
  query endpoint, signed per request with NIP-98; live updates exist only on the WebSocket. The client
  has to merge both without counting a reply twice.
- **Signing per request.** NIP-98 (HTTP) and Blossom media downloads need a fresh signature for every
  request. With the app's own key that is invisible; with Amber it is one approval per request unless the
  user allows these kinds permanently in Amber.
- **Quartz in tests.** The app's Nostr library is built for Java 21 and cannot load in the Java 17
  unit-test runtime, so the integration test signs through the JVM build of the same secp256k1 library.
- **What the Buzz apps do on the client side.** After an invite, the Buzz apps create or join two starter
  rooms themselves. We left that out on purpose; upstream has since moved it to the server (default rooms).

## Time to the first room list on the Nokia

Measured from the moment the client starts (a member opening Community), on the test phone over
Wi-Fi, build 6:

| Start | Relay info | Signed in | Room directory | Memberships | My rooms | First room list |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| App restarted, server address cached | 0.49 s | 0.49 s | 0.77 s | 0.80 s | 0.88 s | **0.91 s** |
| First lookup of the server's address | 5.55 s | 5.61 s | 5.99 s | 6.03 s | 6.13 s | **6.17 s** |

The 5.5 s in the second row are the phone's first DNS lookup on this network: any name it has not
looked up yet takes about as long (measured on other hosts too). Builds 2 and 3, which fetched the
relay info before signing in instead of next to it, needed 6.2 and 6.5 s. On the CI runner the
first room list comes after 0.07 to 0.12 s. Phase 1's offline cache would show the last room list
at once and refresh it in the background.

## Effort per phase

Working days of implementation with tests and a device check per step, not counting review rounds.

| Phase | Content | Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Foundation | reactions, edits and deletes from our side; mentions with a member picker; unread state (local, then NIP-RS sync); older pages and thread windows; an offline cache in its own encrypted database file | 8–12 days |
| 2 Complete | forum votes; image, video and file upload and download (Blossom auth, metadata stripping, previews); voice messages (record, play); search; direct messages; canvases | 12–18 days |
| 3 Notifications without Google | UnifiedPush on the client and push leases with the relay: 4–6 days. A small push gateway next to the relay that delivers to UnifiedPush endpoints: 5–8 days, plus running it | 9–14 days |
| 4 Huddles | Buzz's own audio protocol over WebSocket (Opus, jitter buffer), microphone permissions, foreground service | 10–15 days, highest risk |
| 5 CruxCoach extras | install a test build from #builds; bug report with version, device and logs filled in (needs phase 2 uploads); climb, session and board cards; open a feature's room from the feature | 8–10 days |

## Upkeep

Upstream Buzz had 323 commits in the 11 days after the pinned version (about 29 a day; 294 in the 30 days
before). 165 touched the phone app, 64 the relay, 20 the protocol documents. None of them changed what the
spike uses on the wire; the client-relevant ones add flags (unlisted rooms, default rooms, more invite
error codes) or reserve future queries (compact membership discovery). Details: PROTOCOL.md §5.

Estimate: before each server update, half a day to a day to read the protocol and route changes in the
areas the client uses, and half a day to two days to bump the pinned version in CI and fix what the
integration test finds. With a monthly server update that is about 1.5–3 days a month, more once media,
DMs and huddles are in.

## Risks

- **Moving target.** Upstream changes daily. The pinned server, the integration test and a protocol review
  before each server update keep this manageable, but every phase adds surface that can drift.
- **Amber and per-request signatures.** NIP-98 and Blossom downloads need a signature per request. Without
  a permanent permission in Amber, image-heavy rooms would prompt constantly. Mitigation: cache media,
  use WebSocket reads where possible, and explain the Amber setting once.
- **Huddles** use Buzz's own audio protocol (Opus over WebSocket). It is the costliest phase and the one
  most likely to change upstream.
- **Notifications** need a component next to the server (a UnifiedPush gateway). That is ops work and a
  new service to keep up to date, unless it can live upstream.
- **App size and focus.** The client must stay a separate part of the app (its own package, its own
  storage file, off by default) so that it never slows down the climbing features.
- **Invite links** open the app only after the user picks it in Android's chooser, unless
  `community.cruxcoach.org` serves an `assetlinks.json` for the app (a server change).

## Where we can do better than the Buzz phone app

- **Forums in the room list**, grouped with rooms, with topics and comment counts.
- **Sign in with Amber** or the app's key; no key import, no second account.
- **No Google needed:** installable from our own channels, and notifications through UnifiedPush (phase 3).
- **Rules first:** the rules and privacy notice are shown in full before joining, and the app joins no
  room on its own.
- **Announce rooms are clear:** a room where new members can only read says so before you join.
- **CruxCoach context:** test builds, bug reports with diagnostics, climb and session cards, and a
  feature's room one tap away from the feature (phase 5).

## Recommendation

Go on, one phase at a time, and decide again after each phase:

1. **Phase 1 next**, still behind the preview switch and in feature builds only. It makes the client
   usable day to day (reactions, edits, mentions, unread state, older history, an offline cache) without
   new server parts.
2. **Keep the pin.** The client targets the server's exact version; the integration test runs against
   that version in CI. A server update becomes one pull request: bump the pin, read the protocol
   changes, fix what the test finds.
3. **Decide notifications early.** Phase 3 needs a component next to the server. Whether that should live
   upstream, next to our server or not at all decides how much of phase 2 is worth it on its own.
4. **Huddles last.** They cost the most, change the most upstream and matter least for a climbing
   community; revisit after phase 3.

## Choices made in the code (easy to change)

- **Names:** someone without a profile shows as a short npub; your own messages show as "You".
- **Rules:** the accept button stays off until the rules text is on screen (at most 3 s).
- **Background:** the connection closes a minute after the app leaves the screen. The minute covers a
  trip to Amber for a signature; there are no notifications in the preview anyway.
- **Server key:** room lists and room notices count only when the server's own key (NIP-11 `self`)
  signed them. A server that does not publish its key gets no room list; the app keeps asking.
- **Thread view:** opens once its first message is loaded (it gives up after 8 s and shows the replies).

## Decisions (maintainer, 2026-11-07)

The questions from this report, answered:

1. **Starter rooms:** the server's default rooms. The server already adds new members to the starter
   rooms, so the app joins nothing by itself.
2. **Direct messages:** Buzz DM rooms first, and the app says clearly whether the server can read
   them. NIP-17 later for end-to-end private messages.
3. **Amber:** yes, with an explanation in the app, and only for the kinds the client needs (sign-in,
   NIP-98 HTTP auth, Blossom media), never "sign everything".
4. **Invite links:** Android's app chooser for now, `assetlinks.json` before a wider release. It must
   list every certificate the app is signed with, also after the planned release key change.
5. **Server updates:** the server and the client's pinned version move together, in one step after
   phase 1.
6. **Phase 1:** go, in feature builds only, behind the preview switch. No Google services anywhere;
   notifications later through UnifiedPush.
7. **Moderation:** reports and removing messages first.
8. **Names:** ask for a display name at joining, prefilled from the profile.
9. **Background connection:** close it a minute after leaving the app, until notifications come.
10. **Cross-check:** in Buzz Desktop everything looks as expected. The thread has its two replies,
    the forum topic its two comments, the sender shows as a short npub, and the malformed reply from
    build 2 does not show at all.

Phase 1 is tracked in [#73](https://github.com/CruxCoach/CruxCoach/issues/73).
